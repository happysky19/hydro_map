# Historical meteorological forcing in hydrological practice

Primary-source review, checked 2026-10-06. Scope: daily forcing for 26 selected Columbia River catchments spanning the United States and Canada. The examples below document particular implementations. A hydrological algorithm does not prescribe a unique meteorological dataset, and use in a published study does not establish that a product is best for these catchments.

## Seven documented modeling examples

| Study or system | Model and version information | Historical forcing, resolution, and period | Region, interpretation, and source location |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Walters et al. (2026), NWRFC calibration framework | NWSRFS SNOW-17, SAC-SMA, UNIT-HG and Lag-K; additional components where needed. No numerical executable release is specified in the inspected methods. | AORC; hourly, 30 arcseconds (0.008333°). Basin/zone averages of precipitation, temperature, and precipitation-type probability derived using humidity and pressure; temperature also drives PET. AORC availability starts in 1979; the three detailed examples use a 1980–2022 calibration record. | Direct Pacific Northwest precedent, including Middle Fork Flathead. Monthly basin-specific forcing adjustments are part of calibration. Daymet and ERA-derived products in Table 3 constrain climatology; they are not the primary time-varying forcing. The paper cites the AORC v1.1 description but does not give an immutable archive identifier. [Paper, §§2, 3.1, 4.7, Table 3, §7, pp. 5–6, 11–12, 15](https://repository.library.noaa.gov/view/noaa/73306/noaa_73306_DS1.pdf). DOI: 10.1111/1752-1688.70112. |
| NOAA National Water Model retrospective | NWM v3.0; distinguish this simulation from the operational forecast configurations. | AORC v1.1 over the complete retrospective period, February 1979–January 2023. Native AORC is hourly at 30 arcseconds; NWM uses its own model grid. | CONUS retrospective with hydrologically contributing territory. No streamflow assimilation is performed in the retrospective. NWM v2.1 instead mixes AORC v1.0 for 1979–2006 and v1.1 for 2007–2020; v2.0/v1.2 used NLDAS. A model name alone therefore does not identify its forcing. NOAA also warns that v3.0 forcing files can carry a misleading “v2.1” metadata tag. [NOAA-managed registry, “Details for Each Version” and “CONUS Meteorological Forcing”](https://registry.opendata.aws/nwm-archive/); [native AORC specification](https://registry.opendata.aws/noaa-nws-aorc/). |
| Kratzert et al. (2021), CAMELS-US multiple-forcing experiment | LSTM ensembles and separately calibrated SAC-SMA/SNOW-17 implementations; archived study code is identified in “Code availability,” rather than a numbered hydrological-model release. | CAMELS basin-mean daily Daymet (1 km native grid), Maurer and NLDAS (both 1/8°). Inputs include precipitation, Tmin/Tmax, vapor pressure and radiation. Train: 1999-10-01–2008-09-30; test: 1989-10-01–1999-09-30. | 531 CONUS benchmark basins. SAC-SMA is calibrated separately with each product; LSTMs receive individual products or combinations. This directly disproves any one-to-one model–forcing rule. These are historical CAMELS releases, not necessarily current Daymet V4 R1. The study supplies extended Maurer/NLDAS Tmin/Tmax and documents timing differences between products. [Paper, §§2.1, 2.3, 2.4, and data/code availability](https://hess.copernicus.org/articles/25/2685/2021/). DOI: 10.5194/hess-25-2685-2021. |
| Ban et al. (2020), western-US warming sensitivity | VIC 4.1.2, specifically 4.1.2g in data availability; also Noah-MP, SAC-SMA and Catchment. | Livneh 2013 (“L13”), daily 1/16° precipitation, Tmin, Tmax and wind, extended from 2011 through 2018. Historical simulation: 1916–2018, with repeated water-year-1915 spin-up. Daily inputs are disaggregated for subdaily models using MTCLIM-related algorithms. | Four western regions including the Columbia. Historical baseline plus imposed temperature perturbations; not a present-day operational forecast. Forcing and parameter consistency mattered: VIC retained the associated L13 parameters. This is an actual Columbia/VIC/Livneh precedent, not evidence that the old forcing archive extends to 2025. [Paper, §3.1, pp. 2–3, and data availability, p. 17](https://repository.library.noaa.gov/view/noaa/64716/noaa_64716_DS1.pdf). DOI: 10.1029/2020WR027158. |
| Mai et al. (2020), Raven structural sensitivity | Raven **3.0**, including an HMETS-emulating structure and other process combinations. | Natural Resources Canada gridded meteorology, approximately 10 km; basin-averaged daily mean temperature and precipitation. Available forcing: 1954–2010. Simulation: 1989–2010, with 1989–1990 excluded as warm-up. | Salmon River near Prince George, British Columbia, outside the Columbia basin. Useful nearby Canadian precedent, but a structural-sensitivity experiment rather than an operational forcing endorsement. The inspected methods identify NRCan, not a numbered forcing-product release. [Paper, §§2.1.2–2.1.3 and Appendix C](https://hess.copernicus.org/articles/24/5835/2020/). DOI: 10.5194/hess-24-5835-2020. |
| Mai et al. (2022), GRIP-GL | Thirteen model configurations, including Raven-based GR4J/HMETS/Blended models, VIC-Raven, MESH-Raven and LSTM; configuration details are in §2.4/Table 1 and supplement. | **RDRS-v2**, hourly, about 10 km, 2000–2017. Inputs are aggregated to each model's requirements; lumped models use daily basin means. Year 2000 is warm-up; calibration 2001–2010; validation 2011–2017. | Transboundary Great Lakes and Ottawa region, 212 gauges. This demonstrates a common cross-border forcing across different model structures. **ERA5-Land SWE is an evaluation reference here, not the meteorological forcing.** The study's v2 record is only 18 years; do not silently substitute the later v2.1 extension. [Paper, §§2.2, 2.4–2.5 and Fig. 2](https://hess.copernicus.org/articles/26/3537/2022/). DOI: 10.5194/hess-26-3537-2022. |
| Arsenault et al. (2023), ungauged-basin comparison | HSAMI, HMETS, GR4J-CemaNeige and a regional LSTM; study implementations, with model structures and code repository identified rather than common release numbers. | Daily **ERA5 extracted through HYSETS**, 1979–2018: rainfall, snowfall, Tmin and Tmax. ERA5 atmospheric native resolution is approximately 31 km, distributed commonly at 0.25°; basin extraction is not a new fine-resolution analysis. | 148 northeastern North American catchments, each with at least 30 years of available flow. Hydrological models use the full available 1979–2018 period with initial warm-up for spatial regionalization experiments. This is evidence for ERA5 usage, **not ERA5-Land usage**. [Paper, §2 and §§3.1, 3.3](https://hess.copernicus.org/articles/27/139/2023/); [ERA5 resolution and daily aggregation methods, Tarek et al. (2020), §§3.1–3.2](https://hess.copernicus.org/articles/24/2527/2020/). DOI: 10.5194/hess-27-139-2023. |

Columbia planning corroboration: RMJOC-II used Livneh-forced historical VIC/PRMS baselines (VIC_P1/P2/P3 and PRMS_P1), with a water-year 1976–2005 baseline in the reservoir-operations assessment. These historical baselines must be distinguished from bias-corrected climate-model simulations of historical/future weather. [RMJOC-II Part II, §§4–4.1, printed p. 30](https://www.bpa.gov/-/media/Aep/power/hydropower-data-studies/rmjoc-ll-report-part-ll.PDF). The official indexed report text was available; the browser PDF renderer could not open the complete document during this review.

HYSETS itself is a multi-source **dataset**, not a hydrological model. It provides alternatives including ERA5 and ERA5-Land, NRCan and Livneh; an application's use of HYSETS does not identify which forcing it selected. Its existing gauge catchment means also do not automatically correspond to the 26 project-local polygons. [Arsenault et al. (2020), abstract and meteorological-data methods](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41597-020-00583-2).

## Implications for a 30-year Columbia archive

| Candidate | Verified useful coverage | Main practical qualification |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **AORC v1.1** | Hourly, 30 arcseconds, from 1979; CONUS plus contributing areas. Documented valid native extent: 25–53°N, 125–67°W. | It is not US-only. Verify valid cells over every full catchment, especially northern headwaters. The NOAA registry still displays an April 2, 2026 notice about incorrectly masked rows and Zarr regeneration; completeness requires a real check. [NOAA specification and announcement](https://registry.opendata.aws/noaa-nws-aorc/). |
| **Daymet V4 R1** | Daily, 1 km, continental North America from 1980; the current NASA record extends through 2025. | R1 corrects Canadian-station input problems affecting 2020/2021. Preserve the exact revision. Daymet includes February 29 but omits December 31 in leap years: a complete Gregorian daily series needs an explicit, flagged policy. [NASA product description](https://data.nasa.gov/dataset/daymet-daily-surface-weather-data-on-a-1-km-grid-for-north-america-version-4-r1-b1d6b), [calendar and variables](https://daymet.ornl.gov/overview). |
| **ERA5-Land** | Global, hourly, 1950 onward; 9 km native resolution and 0.1° distributed grid. | Its atmospheric forcing comes from ERA5. Precipitation is an interpolated forcing field, so the 9 km land grid does not establish independently resolved 9 km rainfall skill. Native hourly accumulations must be handled according to their forecast-step convention. [CDS product](https://cds.climate.copernicus.eu/datasets/reanalysis-era5-land?tab=overview), [ECMWF documentation, “Accumulations” and parameter tables](https://confluence.ecmwf.int/pages/viewpage.action?pageId=505384848). |

**Recommendation (inference from these precedents):** Start with AORC v1.1 as the primary candidate if all 26 polygons pass spatial and temporal coverage checks: it has the most direct NWRFC/NWM precedent and supplies the atmospheric variables needed beyond daily precipitation and temperature. Keep Daymet V4 R1 as the independent precipitation/temperature comparison and ERA5-Land as a consistent cross-border sensitivity dataset or alternative where complete atmospheric inputs are needed. Compare held-out hydrological performance before selecting the production forcing. A finer output grid alone is not a quality ranking.

The current extraction candidate uses **1995–2024** so that the required AORC precipitation boundary at 2025-01-01 00Z is available. A latest-30-years window of 1996–2025 still requires the missing 2026-01-01 00Z boundary in the inspected archives. This is an extraction decision, not a model requirement. The older studies' ending years should not be mistaken for present product availability.

RDRS remains a relevant Canadian comparison: the producer paper describes **RDRS-v2.1 for 1980–2018**, with a snow-density fix, distinct from the GRIP-GL v2 release. It supports some 30-year historical windows but does not by itself cover 1996–2025. Current release availability and any later extensions were not established here. [Gasset et al. (2021), data availability](https://hess.copernicus.org/articles/25/4917/2021/index.html).

Before comparing products, use identical area-weighted project polygons and daily boundaries; record source versions, units, variable definitions, missing-day policy and any corrections. Retain local and total catchment meanings. In particular, define whether Tmin/Tmax means extrema of the basin-mean hourly temperature or basin means of grid-cell daily extrema; those operations are not equivalent. This is a processing design choice, not a claim from the case studies.

The principal unresolved uncertainties are high-elevation precipitation and snow biases, exact archive completeness, and compatibility with calibration targets. Regulated dam releases do not directly validate unregulated catchment runoff without accounting for storage, routing and diversions. Columbia climate-model studies explicitly distinguish these targets. [Queen et al. (2021), §2.3](https://hess.copernicus.org/articles/25/257/2021/).
